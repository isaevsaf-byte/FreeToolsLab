£5,918 a year evaporates when you move a supplier from 30 to 60 days.

Not saved by you. Not earned by the supplier. Gone.

Here is the mechanism.
Longer terms are a loan. The supplier lends you 30 days of spend.
On £1.2m a year that is £98,630, parked on your side.
You value it at your cost of capital. Say 6%. Worth £5,918 a year.
The supplier funds the gap at its rate. Say 12%. Costs £11,836 a year.
The difference leaves the pair. Nobody books it.

For a £4m supplier that is 3.7% of its profit.
Nine days of its sales, frozen, to save you 0.5% of spend.

One principle: never price a terms change at one rate.
Borrowed money costs what the borrower pays, not what the lender saves.

The same maths shows the fix.
Any early-payment discount between 0.49% and 0.99% beats the extension.
For both sides.
